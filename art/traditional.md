<p>
Traditional art has always been my foundation for learning composition, contrast, and balance.  
Most of these works are created with alcohol markers, fineliners, or coloured inks.  
They represent moments of study, experimentation, and quiet exploration of texture, anatomy, and style.
</p>

<hr>

<h1>✏️ Fineliner Drawings</h1>
<p>
These pieces are drawn primarily with black fineliners and coloured accents, exploring depth, perspective, and architectural details.  
I often take inspiration from real-world textures—stone walls, foliage, and wooden patterns—to create a sense of atmosphere and place.  
Alongside original sketches, I also enjoy studying and copying from great manga panels, since they demonstrate masterful use of line weight, shading, and motion.  
These drawings are a mix of observation, technique study, and pure appreciation for the craft of ink art.
</p>

<h1>🎨 Anatomy & Colour Studies</h1>
<p>
This series focuses on anatomy, fabric flow, and the use of layered colour to build visual rhythm.  
These characters were inspired by East Asian historical clothing and classical figure drawing references.  
Working traditionally helped me understand how markers and pigments interact to create gradients and shadow depth without relying on digital blending.  
Each piece was drawn freehand and refined through careful layering of colour tones and highlights.
</p>

<h1>🐸 Alcohol Marker Animals</h1>
<p>
These bright and stylized animal portraits were created using alcohol markers and fineliners.  
The focus here was to balance simplicity and realism while maintaining expressive colour palettes and clean contours.  
Each animal reflects a small exploration of colour harmony—contrasting warm and cool tones to bring the forms to life.  
This set represents a playful exercise in improving marker blending and achieving smooth colour transitions.
</p>

<h1>🧸 Fun Doodles & Experiments</h1>
<p>
These lighthearted sketches capture moments of spontaneous creativity and comfort drawing.  
They include simple characters and cheerful designs—pieces that remind me to have fun while learning technique.  
Often, these doodles are small exercises in expression, composition, and colour testing before moving to larger works.  
They represent the imaginative side of my traditional art practice, where precision gives way to playfulness.
</p>
