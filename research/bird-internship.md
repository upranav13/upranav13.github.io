## Understanding territory ranges in newly discovered bird species

During my undergraduate studies, I participated in a field-based internship focused on mapping the **territory ranges of two newly described bird species** from the montane forests of Kodaikanal, India — an ecological hotspot rich in endemic avifauna.  
The project aimed to document the spatial distribution and habitat use of these species to support ongoing **biodiversity monitoring and conservation planning**.

---

## Research objectives

- Map the breeding and foraging territories of two newly discovered bird species.  
- Examine habitat characteristics and interspecies overlap in a high-altitude environment.  
- Contribute to baseline data for long-term ecological monitoring in the Western Ghats region.

---

## Methods in brief

Fieldwork was conducted across multiple forest patches, using **point-count surveys**, **direct behavioral observations**, and **GPS-based mapping** to estimate territory boundaries.  
Data were collected over several weeks during the breeding season, recording **song perches, feeding sites, and nesting activity**.  
The survey design was developed in coordination with local researchers and ornithologists to ensure consistent sampling effort across sites.

---

## Key observations

The results revealed clear, **non-overlapping territories** for both species, with ranges influenced by forest density and understory composition.  
Individuals exhibited strong site fidelity, returning repeatedly to the same foraging areas and singing posts.  
These findings provided the first quantitative estimates of territory size and spatial organization for these newly described taxa.

---

## Conclusion

The study provided initial insights into the **territorial organization and habitat use of two newly described bird species** in the montane forests of Kodaikanal. Both species maintained distinct territories, with individuals showing strong site fidelity to particular foraging areas and song perches. Territory distributions also appeared to vary with local habitat characteristics, providing baseline information on the spatial ecology of these recently described taxa.

This internship was my first hands-on experience with **field ecology and behavioral research**, introducing me to systematic wildlife surveys, behavioral observations, ecological data collection, and spatial mapping. It provided an early foundation in designing and carrying out field-based research and helped broaden my experience with studying biological questions directly in natural populations.
