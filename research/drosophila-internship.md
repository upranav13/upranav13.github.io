## Experimental assays on stress response and dispersal evolution

As part of a research internship, I worked on a **Drosophila experimental evolution project** investigating how dispersal history influences behavioral and reproductive performance under stress.  
The project examined whether lines selected for increased dispersal capacity show **enhanced tolerance to environmental stressors**, providing insights into the relationship between movement ecology, physiology, and adaptation.

---

## Research objectives

- Test whether dispersal-selected populations evolve greater resilience to **chemical and physical stressors**.  
- Quantify behavioral and reproductive changes following stress exposure.  
- Explore potential links between **stress tolerance** and **evolution of dispersal traits**.

---

## Methods in brief

We used **base** and **dispersal-selected lines** of *Drosophila melanogaster* maintained under laboratory conditions for multiple generations.  
Two stress treatments were applied:
1. **Chemical stress** – exposure to caffeine to induce mild physiological stress.  
2. **Physical stress** – periodic vibration to simulate disturbance.

After exposure, we measured several **behavioral and life-history traits**, including activity levels, development time, mating duration, and reproductive success.  
Comparisons were made between stressed and control flies across both line types.

---

## Key observations

Dispersal-selected lines showed **greater reproductive performance and activity** under both stress treatments compared to base populations.  
While development time remained largely unaffected, evolved flies exhibited higher **resilience and recovery rates** following disturbance.  
These results indicate that selection for dispersal may indirectly enhance stress tolerance through physiological or behavioral co-adaptation.

## Reflection

This project introduced me to **experimental evolution and behavioral assays**, bridging ecology, genetics, and physiology.  
It strengthened my skills in data collection, statistical analysis, and experimental design, and gave me an appreciation for how **selection on movement and stress resistance** can jointly shape evolutionary trajectories.  
The experience directly complemented my later PhD work on **genotype-by-environment interactions and fitness variation**.
