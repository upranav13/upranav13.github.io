## Investigating the 6Pgdh polymorphism in the wild

Natural populations of the bulb mite *Rhizoglyphus robini* show a stable polymorphism at the **6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase (6Pgdh)** locus, even though one allele (S) tends to fix quickly in laboratory settings. This persistence in nature suggests that environmental heterogeneity — such as variation in temperature, soil chemistry, and microhabitat conditions — may be maintaining genetic diversity through balancing selection.

This field study, conducted across multiple regions in Poland, examined how geographic, seasonal, and environmental factors influence 6Pgdh allele frequencies. Sampling was carried out in both summer and autumn, allowing comparisons across time as well as space.

---

## Research objectives

- Determine the prevalence of 6Pgdh polymorphism across Poland, testing for broad spatial patterns in allele frequencies.  
- Assess seasonal stability, comparing allele distributions between summer and autumn populations.  
- Identify environmental correlates, linking allele frequencies to macroclimatic (temperature, precipitation) and local abiotic variables (soil composition, cation concentrations).

---

## Methods in brief

Mite populations were sampled from agricultural and semi-natural habitats across Poland. Climatic data (temperature, precipitation) were retrieved from regional datasets, while soil samples were analyzed for organic content and cation concentrations (Na⁺, K⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺). Genotyping at the 6Pgdh locus was performed using enzyme electrophoresis.

Statistical analyses tested for correlations between allele frequencies and environmental variables using **generalized linear models (GLMs)** and **redundancy analyses (RDA)** to capture multi-variable relationships.

## Interpretation

The results provide strong evidence that environment-dependent selection contributes to maintaining the 6Pgdh polymorphism in the wild. The consistent association with soil cations and environmental PCs suggests physiological or metabolic underpinnings linked to energy metabolism. Moreover, the absence of strong seasonal shifts implies that the selective environment remains relatively stable across short timescales.

These findings establish the ecological context for the subsequent **laboratory experiments**, which test how temperature and sexual selection interact to shape 6Pgdh dynamics under controlled conditions.
